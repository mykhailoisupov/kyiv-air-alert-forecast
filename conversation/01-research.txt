**Main conclusions**
- **Workhorse model:** a global gradient-boosted tree model on lag, calendar, spell-length and neighbour-state features is the best-supported primary model. This rests on M5 and the forecasting-with-trees literature, plus my inference that 25 regions is too small a panel for high-capacity deep models.
- **Deep and graph models:** there is no evidence they win at this scale. Linear and MLP baselines have repeatedly matched Transformers and spatio-temporal GNNs on much larger benchmarks.
- **Foundation models:** Chronos-2, TiRex and TimesFM-2.5 clearly beat classical statistical baselines zero-shot. On fev-bench, Chronos-2 has a 90.7% win rate and 47.3% skill score against 20.2% skill for a statistical ensemble. Those benchmarks contain little zero-inflated or binary data, so treat these models as challengers on daily aggregates, not presumed winners.
- **Statistical core:** negative-binomial autoregression with neighbour terms for daily counts, and a dynamic logistic / discrete-time hazard model for the on/off state. The hazard formulation unifies the Markov, survival and classifier views in one feature table.
- **Point processes:** a multivariate Hawkes model over region marks with a seasonal background is the natural advanced interpretable model for onsets. It needs explicit handling of three mismatches: no onsets while an alert is active, simultaneous country-wide onsets, and inflated self-excitation if the background is held constant.
- **Neural point processes:** not supported. EasyTPP finds no single winner and poor event-time prediction across models; EarthquakeNPP finds none of five neural models beat ETAS.
- **Text and LLMs:** context can help substantially (Context is Key: best LLM 0.159 vs Chronos 0.326), but with occasional catastrophic failures. ForecastBench reported top systems statistically indistinguishable from superforecasters in July 2026, a claim that is contested. Temporal leakage is the dominant failure mode, so historical LLM backtests on pre-cutoff periods are invalid. My recommendation is to use LLMs as feature extractors first.
- **Evaluation:** the key design choice is a target persistence cannot solve: onset within a horizon given no current alert, termination, or state at a lead longer than typical durations.
  - **Protocol:** rolling-origin backtests over multiple disjoint periods with purge and embargo.
  - **Baselines:** persistence, same hour last week, and a trailing hour-of-week climatology per region.
  - **Metrics:** proper scores, with Brier skill computed within region and time-of-day strata, and everything reported per lead time.
  - **Evidence:** in the VIEWS conflict challenge, trailing-window empirical baselines were hard to beat, and most models lost to a no-change baseline when predicting changes.
- **Predictability limits:** this is my inference, not a measured result. Persistence, short-lead propagation and base rates are predictable; the timing of new attacks and tactic shifts is not, from alert history alone. Adaptation methods (windowed or decayed retraining, drift detectors, adaptive conformal) only react after a change is observed.
