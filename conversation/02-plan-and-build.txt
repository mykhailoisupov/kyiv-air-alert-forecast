# Plan: Kyiv air raid alert analysis and onset forecast (4-hour course project)

## Context

Course assignment: a time series analysis of air raid alerts in Ukraine. The brief requires the most recent data, evaluation on the latest period, and no leakage (nothing trained on or used as input may come from after forecast time). Text/media data is optional. Deliverables: GitHub repo with working Python code, a full AI conversation log, and a ~100-word reflection (what went wrong, how the approach changed, why the final version beats the first).

The research in `reports/Ukraine air raid alert forecasting.md` found that most existing projects fail on exactly the brief's criteria: stale data (the common Vadimkin official file froze on 7 Sep 2026), random splits, and targets that an already-running alert answers by itself. Leakage-controlled projects get only +2% to +15% Brier skill over hour-of-day climatology for alert onsets at 1-6 h. So the project competes on data recency and evaluation quality, not model complexity.

Budget is about 4 hours, so the base is deliberately narrow and the text work is a stretch.

## Scope decisions

- **Region: Kyiv City only.** Its open-data file is authoritative, needs no token, is current to 12 Oct 2026, has no duplicates or over-24h events, carries yellow/red level and cause since the 7 Sep 2026 reform, and Kyiv City stayed one alert unit through the 2025 oblast-to-raion change. The current national alternative (Vadimkin volunteer file) has 5% imputed end times and under-records Kyiv. Code keeps a `region` column so a panel can be added later.
- **Target: alert onset.** "Given no alert is active at time t, does a new one start within the next H hours?" for H = 1, 3, 6. A 24 h horizon is expected to saturate (Kyiv had about 6 alerts a day in Sep 2026); the base-rate table will confirm before it is dropped.
- **Test period: the latest 8 weeks before the data snapshot**, walk-forward with weekly refits, with the post-reform weeks (from 7 Sep 2026) also reported separately.
- **No LLM in the base.** See Stretch for the one place it could go.

## Data

- Primary: `https://data.kyivcity.gov.ua/dataset/bb0f27d3-00e2-414a-9d6e-a8b6633f8a2a/resource/5e4fb8a8-f0c8-4a12-885f-192d1f0dba75/download/airalerts.json` (about 2,641 records from 28 Feb 2022; fields `dateTimeStart`, `dateTimeEnd`, `level`, `cause`). Refreshed on Mondays.
- Top-up to the present: `https://kyiv.digital/open-api/air-alert/history` (start/all-clear events with causes; request JSON). Depth and schema are unverified; if it surprises, use the weekly file alone and state the snapshot date.
- Cleaning rules: timestamps are Kyiv local with no offset, so localize to `Europe/Kyiv` (handle DST gaps and repeats, log how many rows were affected), store UTC; map the string `"null"` level to missing; sort, assert no overlaps and end > start; keep an ongoing alert as open-ended.

1	# Marks & anatomy
2	
3	The quiet, considered look is a few fixed specs plus two pieces of negative space.
4	The data is the only thing allowed to be loud.
5	
6	## Mark specs (fixed across every chart)
7	
8	| Mark | Spec |
9	|---|---|
10	| Bar / column | **<= 24px thick** (cap it - never fill the slot; let the band's leftover be air); **4px rounded data-end, square at the baseline**; grows from a single baseline |
11	| Line | **2px**, round join/cap |
12	| Marker / end-dot | **>= 8px** (r >= 4), filled with the series color |
13	| Area fill | the series hue at **~10% opacity** (a wash, never a saturated block) |
14	| Gridlines / axes | one-step-off-surface gray, **hairline (1px), solid** (never dashed), recessive |
15	
16	## The two spacers (white doing the separating)
17	
18	- **Surface gap.** A **2px gap** in the surface color separates touching marks - every
19	  segment of a stacked bar, and every adjacent (touching) bar, the same width. Keep it
20	  one consistent width across a stack; neighbors one step apart read distinct because of
21	  the gap, not a stroke drawn around them.
22	- **Surface ring.** Dots and end-markers carry a **2px ring in the surface color**,
23	  so they stay legible where they cross a line or overlap each other. The ring is part
24	  of the mark's hover/hit target, not just spacing - see `interaction.md` (small dots
25	  are easy to under-size for hover).
26	
27	Never draw a border around a mark to separate it. The gap and the ring are the
28	mechanism; a stroke adds data-weight ink that isn't data.
29	
30	## Labels & legend
31	
32	A **legend is always present for two or more series** - the dependable identity
33	channel; never make the reader rely on color-matching alone. Direct labels then ride
34	the marks to *supplement* it. **A single series needs no legend box**: there is only
35	one color, so the chart's title or subtitle already says what is plotted. A box with
36	one swatch restates the title and costs space.
37	
38	- **Label selectively - never a number on every point.** A value beside every dot or
39	  segment is chaos and goes unread. Label the endpoint, the extreme, or the one series
40	  the story is about; let the axis, the legend, and the tooltip/table carry the rest.

1	# Anti-patterns - what goes wrong
2	
3	Check every chart against this list. If your output matches an entry, it is wrong -
4	fix it before shipping. These are real failure modes, each caught in shipping
5	dashboards.
6	
7	## Color & encoding
8	
9	**Bad: Dual-axis charts (two y-scales on one plot).**
10	Why it misleads: the alignment of the two scales is arbitrary, so the chart invents a
11	correlation that isn't in the data. Real example: an "Adoption" chart plotting Users
12	(0-30k) against Sessions (0-800k) - a reviewer flagged it as looking "hallucinated."
13	Good: Do instead: two charts, small multiples, or index both series to a common base
14	(=100 at t0) on **one** axis.
15	
16	**Bad: Recolor-on-filter.** Assigning colors by current rank, so filtering out a series
17	repaints the survivors.
18	Why: a reader who learned "Acme is blue" is now misled.
19	Good: Color follows the entity, not its row number. Survivors keep their hue.
20	
21	**Bad: Cycling / generating hues past 8.** A 9th categorical color, generated or reused.
22	Why: indistinguishable from an existing slot under CVD; breaks the order check.
23	Good: Fold the tail into "Other," facet into small multiples, or use composite encoding.
24	
25	**Bad: Eyeballing colorblind-safety.** "These look different enough."
26	Good: Run `scripts/validate_palette.js`. Adjacent Delta E >= 8 (OKLab ×100), or 6-8 WITH secondary encoding.
27	
28	**Bad: A value-ramp on nominal categories.** Coloring each bar darker-where-bigger
29	when the categories have no natural order (products, teams, endpoints).
30	Why: it double-encodes bar length as hue, burns the only free channel on
31	information the chart already shows, and fails the categorical checks by design
32	(a ramp spans the lightness band and drops below the chroma floor).
33	Good: One series -> one color (slot 1) for every bar. Ordered categories (funnel,
34	tiers, age bands) -> the ordinal ramp, validated with `--ordinal`.
35	
36	**Bad: Rainbow / non-neighbor sequential.** A multi-hue ramp for magnitude.
37	Good: One hue, light->dark. (Analogous neighbors or semantic heat are the only multi-hue
38	sequential exceptions, always with a scale legend.)
39	
40	**Bad: A hue at the diverging midpoint, or two cool hues as the two poles.**

## Why logistic regression

I chose it in the plan, before seeing any results, because it fits both the question and the amount of data.

- **It answers the question directly.** The target is yes/no (does a new alert start within H hours?) and the output has to be a probability. With one row per hour and inputs that change over time, logistic regression is the standard model for "chance the event starts in the next step".
- **There is little data in the regime that matters.** A 16-week training window is about 2,500 quiet hours, and the current surge is only about 10 weeks old. A simple model with around 18 inputs is less likely to fit noise than a flexible one.
- **It copes when the alert rate moves outside what it has seen.** Its output keeps moving in the same direction as recent alert counts climb. Tree models cannot forecast beyond the range they were trained on.
- **Earlier work pointed the same way.** In the leakage-controlled projects from the research, logistic regression matched or beat LightGBM for alert onsets.
- **It was cheap in a 4-hour budget.** It refits in under a second each week and usually needs no separate calibration step.

The run backed up the third point: boosted trees on the same inputs averaged a 12% forecast at 1 hour when 33% of hours had a new alert, and scored 18% to 86% worse than climatology.

Three limits on that choice:

- **It is not shown to be the best model.** The only alternative I tried was one boosted-tree setup with default settings, so the result holds for that configuration, not for tree models in general.
- **Its edge over the simplest rules is small.** Over the 8 weeks it beats the best simple forecast by 3% to 5%, with intervals that include zero.
- **Its probabilities are only roughly calibrated.** Bin by bin they are uneven, and its highest 1-hour forecasts were too high.

Because Kyiv was the only place where I could get data that was current, complete and consistently defined within a 4-hour budget. It was a data decision, not a judgment that Kyiv is the most interesting region.

- **The national official feed is dead.** The official Telegram channel and the dataset built from it stopped on 7 September 2026, so they miss the last five weeks, which is exactly the period your brief says to evaluate on.
- **The national alternatives are weaker or slow to get.** The only current national file is oblast-level, has 5% of end times filled in as "start + 30 minutes", and under-records Kyiv. The alerts.in.ua API needs a token requested through a form and only returns one month of history.
- **Kyiv's city API had none of those problems.** It needs no key, is current to the minute, goes back to February 2022, and matched the city's published file on all 2,641 alerts.
- **Kyiv is one alert unit for the whole period.** Elsewhere, alerts switched from oblast level to raion level during 2025, which multiplies event counts and breaks any long series. Kyiv City never changed.
- **It carries the new alert levels.** Each alert since the 7 September reform lists its causes (drone, missile, ballistic), which the frozen national file does not have.

The cost of that choice:

- **Narrower than the assignment title.** The brief says "Ukraine"; this covers one city. If graders expect national coverage, that is the main gap.
- **Fewer events, wider intervals.** One city in a 10-week-old surge is why most margins over the simple rules include zero.
- **No cross-region signal.** The model cannot use an alert in a neighbouring oblast as a warning for Kyiv.
- **Results do not transfer.** Front-line oblasts are under alert most of the time and western ones mostly follow nationwide alerts, so Kyiv's numbers say little about them.

If you want to widen it, the code already keeps a `region` column. The quick route is adding the oblast-level volunteer file as a second, lower-quality panel; the better route is requesting an alerts.in.ua token and archiving from now on.
